My Website Is A Shifting House - August 8, 2026

Laura Schwulst is an American designer, artist, and writer. She was born in 1988 and was around 30 years old in 2018 when she wrote My Website Is A Shifting House. Schwulst uses different objects as metaphors for what web can be. She encourages web designers to explore different uses for websites and to make the web what you want it to be.


A Rant About Technology - August 8, 2026

Ursula K. Le Guin was an American author known for writing speculative fiction. She was born in 1929 and died in 2018. She was around 76 years old in 2005 when she wrote A Rant About Technology. Le Guin claims that current technology advancements overshadow everyday tools as technology. She thinks people should not overlook knives, wheels, clocks, and chairs as technology. Le Guin makes the argument that matches are seen as low-tech, but anyone who’s created fire without matches would see them as a high-tech invention. This article reminds us that websites are not simple and primitive technology. Despite technology making it easier to build websites, they are not simple. A lot of effort goes into website construction and hand-coding reveals the meticulous process of web design.


A Handmade Web - August 27, 2026

J.R. Carpenter is a British-Canadian artist and writer, she is known for working with print, performance, and digital media. She was born in 1972 and was around 42 years old in 2015 when she wrote A Handmade Web. When J.R. says “handmade web” she is referring to web pages coded by hand rather than by software. Handmade web is very personal, rich, and slow. Handmade web contradicts the motive of business and corporate web. Web can perform functions that traditional books are unable to do. In J.R.’s example, her Fishes & Flying Things book has an end, but her web page is continuous. When the handmade web emerged, print and digital media had a closer relationship. The growth of the internet and social media has inspired a sudden return to the version of the internet that was smaller and quieter. This discussion makes me think about how we are too reliant on the internet and the harmful impact of data centers trying to keep up with the growing internet. It is radical to hand-code and self-publish experimental web art.


Against an Increasingly User-Hostile Web - September 15, 2026

Parimal Satyal is a designer and writer who grew up in Nepal and lives in Berlin. He was around 28 years old when he wrote Against an Increasingly User-Hostile Web. The web used to be small and personal, now companies track the websites you visit and they steal your personal information. There are some options to minimize tracking such as Firefox, Ublock, etc. The web has evolved to fuel capitalism and make web-users into consumers.


The Internet's Back-to-the-Land Movement - September 25, 2026

Becca Abbe is a graphic designer and programmer living in New York. She earned a BFA in Graphic Design from RISD. She was around 30 years old when she wrote this article in 2019. In the 1970s, a group of technocrats and business elites predicted that the earth’s resources would be exhausted at the current rate. This pushed for a reversal of growth and sustainability movements. This resulted in people going off grid. Whole Earth 'Lectronic Link (WELL) was a predecessor to the World Wide Web that acted as a communication system that acted outside government and academia. Early on, WELL and WWW were small personal information sharing platforms, but eventually corporate giants took over this technology. The term “cloud” is a marketing buzzword more than an accurate description of how files are stored. There is not an actual “cloud” your information is just being stored on someone else's (Likely Amazon, Google, Facebook, or Apple) computer. Similarly to how people went off-grid in the 1970s to reverse negative impact on the environment, there are communities today that push for a return to a smaller and more personal web. These communities want to opt out of mainstream internet providers and host content on local computers. This is the return to peer-to-peer web.


The Good Room - November 12, 2026

Frank Chimero is a New York-based designer and was around 35 years old when he wrote The Good Room. Chimero recalls hearing that a library is one of the few remaining places that cares more about you than your wallet. This resonated with me for a few reasons. I am generally very frugal, I try to resist making unnecessary purchases because I dislike waste and I dislike only being seen as a consumer. I also feel like the GDES curriculum pushes students in the direction of fine artists rather than towards design jobs at companies that care most about people’s wallets. This handmade web class has also made me think more about putting people before profits, as profit has very negatively impacted the web. Chimero recommends that we enable our freedom of choice by spending more time in places/webspaces that are lively and nourishing. There is a need for both commercial and non-commerical spaces, but it is important to have a balance that works well for you. He also talks about how people should figure out a personal set-up that works well for them. Understanding your own values is important to create a set-up or system that fits your needs. This might diverge from the reading a bit, but my “set-up” involves avoiding buying products that are packaged with lots of unnecessary plastic/plastic bags, buying more from smaller and locally owned businesses rather than corporations, buying used goods (especially clothes) instead of buying new, reducing food waste, recycling, prioritizing products that are durable, functional, versatile, convenient, ethical, and fairly priced. It was also interesting to learn that social media promotes extremist content, this was unfortunate to hear and it is new information to me. It was also interesting to me that people who mostly use apps for creative media consumption and scheduling are happier. This reminds me that I should try to be on my phone less and manage the apps that I choose to use.
